### A1 What does the script compute and what signals does it show?
The script splits each bar's volume into buy and sell volume by comparing every realtime tick with the previous close (lines 13–17), accumulating both in `varip` variables that reset on a new bar (lines 6–12). It plots the difference as columns (line 21), its 20-bar SMA as a line (line 22) and a zero level (line 23). Two `alertcondition` calls fire when the smoothed delta crosses zero (lines 24–25); the author presents them as entry (positive) and exit (negative).

### A2 Which signal is known at bar close and can be placed in an alert?
None. Both alerts (lines 24–25) fire on `smooth`, which is built from `buyVol` and `sellVol` — `varip` accumulators that update on every realtime tick (lines 6–8, 13–17). On a historical bar the script runs once, so the split collapses to the whole bar's volume on one side (line 14: `close >= close[1]`); in realtime it is the tick-by-tick split. The two series differ, so a signal computed on history does not describe what the alert will do, and an alert that fired cannot be checked against the chart afterwards. Example: on [[t:2026-03-22T18:00:00Z@89016]] (the highest-volume bar of the last 12 months, 962) the historical delta is −962 — the whole bar on one side — while any realtime split of the same bar lies between −962 and +962. A close-based reformulation would be a different indicator, not this one. **NO: not tradeable** — blocks B and C are skipped.
